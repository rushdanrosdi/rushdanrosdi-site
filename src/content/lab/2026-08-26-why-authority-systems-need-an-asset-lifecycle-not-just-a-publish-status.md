Publishing creates a moment.

Authority systems need a lifecycle.

A page goes live. A LinkedIn post is distributed. A case study is added. A framework is updated.

Operationally, the task looks complete.

But strategically, the asset may only be entering the next stage of its useful life.

## Published does not mean finished

A simple content workflow often uses:

```text
Draft
Review
Published
```

That is enough to get content live.

But a published asset can later become:

- more valuable;
- outdated;
- redundant;
- stronger through new evidence;
- misaligned with current positioning;
- useful for a different buyer question;
- ready to be consolidated with another asset.

That means "Published" should not be the final state.

## Authority assets change in value

An asset may be highly useful when first published.

Then the business changes.

The offer becomes more precise. A new case study appears. A framework evolves. Buyers ask different questions.

The asset may still be accurate, but its strategic role may have changed.

That is why the asset registry needs lifecycle information, not just publication metadata.

## A simple lifecycle model

A practical first version could be:

```text
Create
→ Review
→ Publish
→ Distribute
→ Observe
→ Refresh / Reuse / Consolidate / Retire
```

Publication is not the end.

It is the point where the asset begins producing evidence, distribution signals and feedback.

## Refresh

Refresh when the core purpose is still useful but parts of the asset have become weaker.

Reasons may include:

- outdated examples;
- changed positioning;
- new proof;
- better terminology;
- stronger buyer questions;
- new competitor context;
- framework evolution.

## Reuse

Some assets deserve to support more than one channel or buyer context.

A strong case study may be reused in:

- a service page;
- a proposal;
- a LinkedIn post;
- a buyer objection response;
- a framework example;
- a comparison page.

Reuse extends the value of strong evidence without changing the underlying claim.

## Consolidate

As the library grows, two assets may begin doing almost the same job.

Instead of keeping both alive indefinitely, the better move may be to consolidate.

```text
Asset A
Explains category positioning

Asset B
Explains almost the same positioning with newer evidence

Decision
Merge the strongest parts into one clearer authority asset
```

Consolidation reduces fragmentation.

## Retire

Some assets should eventually stop playing an active role.

Examples include:

- obsolete service pages;
- old positioning;
- outdated technical claims;
- retired offers;
- weak duplicate content;
- old bios;
- content that no longer reflects the intended entity.

Retiring does not always mean deleting.

It may mean redirecting, archiving, removing from navigation, marking it historical or replacing it with a stronger canonical asset.

The system should know the asset is no longer active evidence.

## Lifecycle decisions protect entity clarity

Old assets can keep reinforcing old interpretations.

The business may have repositioned, but the public web may still contain old titles, category labels, service descriptions and case-study language.

If those signals remain active, the entity environment becomes mixed.

Lifecycle management helps reduce that noise.

## Freshness is not only about dates

A recently published page can still be strategically outdated.

A two-year-old page can still be highly useful.

A better review asks:

- Is the core claim still true?
- Does the positioning still match?
- Is the evidence still current?
- Does the buyer question still matter?
- Is there now a stronger asset?
- Should this asset be reused, updated, consolidated or retired?

That is a more meaningful definition of freshness.

## Lifecycle status belongs in the asset registry

Useful fields might include:

```text
Current Status
Last Reviewed
Next Review
Lifecycle Action
Replacement Asset
Reason for Change
```

Example:

```text
Asset:
AI Buyer Discovery Framework v1.1

Current Status:
Current

Lifecycle Action:
Review for v1.2

Reason:
New audit instruments emerging from Lab Notes #020–#025
```

Now the system can see what may need attention next.

## Review cadence should depend on asset type

### Positioning pages

Review when positioning, services, category language or entity interpretation changes.

### Case studies

Review when new results appear, claims become outdated or stronger proof becomes available.

### Frameworks

Review when supporting research accumulates or methodology changes.

### Reference pages

Review when facts, terminology or important external sources change.

The trigger should match the asset's function.

## Lifecycle management improves automation

Once lifecycle fields exist, automation can surface:

- assets overdue for review;
- high-value assets that have not been reused;
- outdated pages still receiving traffic;
- assets with conflicting positioning;
- duplicated assets that should be consolidated;
- proof assets missing from relevant buyer stages.

Without lifecycle data, automation mostly helps publish more.

With lifecycle data, it can help maintain authority quality.

## Lifecycle also makes measurement cleaner

Suppose an asset is refreshed.

That change should be logged.

Then re-testing can compare:

```text
Before Refresh
→ Existing AI framing

Asset Updated
→ clearer positioning + stronger proof

After Refresh
→ re-test buyer questions
```

That ties asset maintenance back to the research loop.

## The commercial implication

Clients do not need a website full of permanently "finished" content.

They need an authority system where important assets stay useful, current and aligned.

Some pages should be refreshed.

Some should be reused.

Some should be merged.

Some should be retired.

The value is not in keeping every page alive forever.

The value is in maintaining the strongest evidence environment.

## The working principle

My current working principle is:

> A published asset is not finished. It should remain active only while it still supports the intended buyer questions, evidence system and entity interpretation.

That is why authority systems need an asset lifecycle, not just a publish status.
